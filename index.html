Guardrails that protect the property

  • Every placement is labelled ADVERT or Sponsored.
  • No advert sits inside the scoreboard rows, and nothing is sold as a rank, a position or a "winner" badge.
  • Providers are shown in random order in the widget, and every provider in the category can be chosen.
  • Placement does not change with votes, and votes do not change with placement.
  • No advertiser is described as the winner unless valid results support it, and then only through the results themselves.
  • Results wording: "valid responses from participating IOL audiences".

Staying top of mind: the 12-month cycle

Voting never closes after the campaign, so a client has a reason to stay visible. The pitch is presence and relevance, never the outcome. Nothing a client buys can influence either winner.

Stage 1 · 7-day campaign

Window presenter, half-page, MPU and sponsored guide while the decision is being made. A campaign winner is announced when the window closes.

Stage 2 · open voting

Stay top of mind

Always-on presence on IOL and the other titles while people keep voting and reading the results content. Results presenter and sponsored guides stay live. Priced as an ongoing presence, not a one-off.

Stage 3 · cycle finale

Close the year

At the end of 12 months a new winner is announced from open voting. A natural moment for a final burst of presence and a year-in-review.

Developer notes

A starting point for Development and Product to validate. The scoreboard maths, thresholds and integrity layers are proposals, not decisions. The widget on this page is the real 300x600 build and it posts a real payload. Each window has at most 5 questions, and every panel on the hub is built from that one list.

Shape of the system

1 · Run configPillar, sub-pillars, 7-day windows, questions, providers, sponsors. Edited in the CMS, no code.
2 · Widget300x600, config-driven. Collects the vote and three answers. Fires an event and posts to the endpoint.
3 · Vote serviceValidates, de-duplicates, scores, stores. The only writer of results.
4 · AggregatesCounts per window, provider and "what matters most" answer. Cached, rebuilt on a short interval.
5 · HubReads aggregates only. Never sees raw votes. Obeys the results mode.

One widget, one config per window

There is no widget per pillar. The widget is built once and hosted on the IOL domain or a CDN with a version number. Each window (for example Insurance, Pet, 1 to 7 March 2027) has its own config. About 50 configs in the first year.

1 · EmbedEvery placement carries only the window ID, for example insurance-pet.
2 · FetchThe widget asks the config store for that window.
3 · RenderQuestions, providers, dates and sponsor line come from the config. Status decides what shows: live, closed or open voting.
4 · VoteEach vote carries the window ID and the config version it was answered against.
5 · ReportAggregates are grouped by window and version. The hub reads these only.

Where each thing is stored

Widget code      CDN / IOL domain, versioned
Window configs   CMS or config store (editable)
Provider master  one list per category, reused
Votes + answers  database, written only by the vote service
Aggregates       cached table, read by the hub
Audit log        every rule, edit and status change

What the team can change, and what is locked

Pillars, sub-pillars and windows are data, not code. Adding "Travel" or a new sub-pillar needs no developer. The questions are the one thing the system itself must lock. This should be enforced by the vote service, not left to people remembering.

ItemDraft or scheduledLiveClosed or open voting
Add or rename a pillar or sub-pillarYesYes, other windows onlyYes, other windows only
Move window datesYesNo, extend only by approvalNo
Questions and answer optionsYesLocked. No edits.Locked. No edits.
Provider listYesAdd only, never removeAdd only
Sponsor line and placementsYesYes, never touches the voteYes
Wording fix (typo)YesNeeds sign-off, logged as a note, not a new versionSame
  • Lock on go-live. When a window goes live its questions are frozen. The vote service rejects any edit and the CMS shows the fields as read-only.
  • A change means a new window. If a question is truly wrong, close the window as void and open a new one. Never edit and carry on, because the votes before and after would not be comparable.
  • Open voting inherits the same questions. Open voters answer the identical questions, so open votes can sit beside the campaign result.
  • Next year's cycle can use new questions. That is a new cycle with its own windows. Do not compare cycle winners across different question sets without saying so.
  • Every change is logged with who, when and what, so the audit trail can answer a challenge.

Scoreboard maths

share(p)     = votes(p) / votes(all)
drivers(p,c) = votes(p AND c) / votes(p)      // why: share of p's voters who put c first
base(c)      = votes(c) / votes(all)
index(p,c)   = drivers(p,c) / base(c) * 100   // 100 = average
filter c     : rank by votes(p AND c) / votes(c)

Show a provider's "why" only if votes(p) >= 100
Show a cell only if      votes(p AND c) >= 30

Thresholds are placeholders. Set them with Data and Product so small samples never read as findings.

Integrity layers (to validate, none guarantees one person, one vote)

  • Server-side rate limits and a session token per window.
  • Bot challenge on submit, and duplicate detection on a hashed signal.
  • Optional sign-in later, which is stronger but changes the data and POPIA picture.
  • Anomaly alerts on spikes for one provider, and an audit log of every rule applied.
  • Invalid votes kept and flagged, never silently dropped, so results can be audited.

Open questions for Development and Product

  • Results mode. The EXCO brief proposed no live leaderboard in the pilot. This prototype has a live, delayed and after-close switch so the team can decide. Live boards invite bandwagon voting and manipulation, so delayed or after-close is the safer start.
  • Where does validation live? A vote service owned by IOL Digital, or a vendor?
  • Provider master list. Who maintains it so every provider can be chosen, and how are "other" write-ins handled?
  • Window lifecycle. Live for 7 days, then the campaign result (frozen and announced), then open voting with no closing date (stored with phase: open and reported separately), then a cycle winner at the end of 12 months. The campaign result is never recalculated. Open questions: whether the cycle winner uses open votes only (recommended, it measures staying power) or all votes; whether a person may vote again in a later cycle; and monthly snapshots of open voting so the trend can be shown.
  • Open voting is easier to game than a 7-day window. With no end date, a provider could keep urging its customers to vote. Rate limits, anomaly alerts and a policy on providers promoting the vote need deciding before launch.
  • Every "why" must be traceable. Each reason on the board is a count of voters' own answers, shown with its numbers (for example, 112 of a provider's 290 voters against 1,100 of 4,300 overall). If anyone questions a reason such as insurer reputation, the exact counts, window and rules applied can be produced from the audit log.
  • Caching. A 30 to 60 second refresh is probably enough for a "live" feel at far lower load.
  • Accessibility and data costs. The widget is a single light file with no external fonts or libraries. Keep it that way.

Content layer: how articles sit around the results

The hub is the index and IOL holds the articles. Content appears in lanes around the scoreboard and never inside it. The headlines below are placeholders to show the structure, not a commissioning list.

Rules for the lanes

  • Before you choose: neutral explainers that name no providers. Live before and during the window, then stay up as evergreen.
  • What Mzansi said: editorial on the results. Opens only after the window closes.
  • Sponsored: labelled, visually separate, and never in the scoreboard rows.
  • No provider-specific content while a window is open for voting.
  • Editorial decides what runs. The hub only displays the tiles.

What data we can collect, and what it is worth

Start with answers that are useful and carry little risk, then earn the right to ask for more. Each step up asks more of the voter and needs a clear reason. All legal points below are questions for Legal and the Information Officer, not conclusions.

The data ladder

Data pointCollectedWhat it is worthRiskWhen

Widget or hub?

Widget = what people say. Their priority, how they buy, how often they compare, the provider they choose. It is short, so every extra question costs completions. Two to four questions is the ceiling.

Hub = what people do. Which windows they visit, which "why" panels they open, which filters they use, which article sent them. This is aggregate behaviour and does not need to identify anyone.

Rule of thumb: ask in the widget only what the scoreboard or a client output will use.

Do not collect

  • Health, medical or claims history, especially around life and funeral cover.
  • Race, religion, politics, sexual orientation, union membership, biometrics.
  • Children's information.
  • ID numbers, bank or policy numbers.
  • Open free text beyond the short "other provider" field, which is capped and screened.
